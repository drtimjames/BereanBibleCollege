# The Day Watch morning briefing pilot

Owner: Dr. Tim James. Prepared 17 November 2026.

## Reader offer
The Day Watch Morning Brief gives readers a compact, sourced account of consequential events. Strategic Development Watch supplies developing coverage and the regional map. The Charlemagne Assessment supplies deeper interpretation, implications and the next evidence to watch.

Pilot delivery: prepare one morning packet daily around 8 a.m. America/Los_Angeles. The existing hourly watch continues its existing work. The morning task prepares Substack and video drafts for Tim. Verified public web editions may be published under Tim's standing authorization. Substack email delivery and YouTube uploads require their own available authorized publishing route.

## Format
- Subject: The Day Watch Morning Brief — [local date]
- Subtitle: a sentence naming the consequential developments covered.
- Opening: one sentence explaining the day's main question.
- Three to five stories. Each gets 50–90 words with dated original sources, what changed, its significance, and the next evidence to watch. Use fewer stories when justified.
- Free/paid boundary in Substack: immediately before The Charlemagne Assessment, after complete free reporting and source links.
- Assessment: 350–600 original words connecting implications and competing explanations, ending with concrete evidence that would change the assessment.
- Scriptural perspective: brief, textually grounded and clearly identified when included.
- Companion video: 2–3 minutes with the same reporting date, source notes and one invitation to the full briefing. Derive it from the checked packet.
- A 30–45 second short can present one verified development and link to the full briefing.

The first web sample is entirely free at news/charlemagne/morning-brief-2026-11-17.html. Its assessment is intentionally shorter than the normal target.

## Editorial production
Research from primary documents and reliable reporting. Attribute intelligence and combat claims to their source. Recheck dates so an old incident cannot appear as today's development. Each live page carries publication and latest substantive update dates. Revise summaries and red badges together. Distinguish a market's timestamp from the briefing date.
Use original prose and direct source links. Avoid unrelated filler. Preserve the military and diplomatic design already used by the Brief. Use the reader promise in public introductions.
Before writing indexes, fetch their latest content and SHA to preserve the hourly watch's edits. Add a digest link, leaving underlying article URLs as the source records. Preserve previous-page navigation and the shared navigation. Verify repository writes and live publication separately.

### Recording script
Good morning. This is The Day Watch for November seventeenth. Three stories deserve attention today.

Bahrain reports intercepting an Iranian attack. Iran's Guards described a radar installation as the target. The next questions concern verified damage and any operational response. The source is Reuters.

In Washington, CNN has reported Iranian attack planning against senior officials. The White House's public response said there was no credible homeland threat at that time. Keep the date and wording of that response alongside the reporting. Watch for a fresh official assessment.

And the FAA continues to investigate Friday's disruption. Its published account describes excessive traffic in its communications network and the stabilization that followed isolation of two sites. We are watching for its final findings.

What connects these stories is the need to ask what the evidence actually establishes. A warning, an interception and a service failure each require their own follow-up. In the Gulf, look for documented damage, shipping restrictions and vessel movement. In Washington, follow public agency assessments and any disclosed court evidence. In aviation, follow the technical findings and the measures adopted to prevent a repeat.

Our deeper Charlemagne Assessment asks what would change the picture. That question helps us remain alert and willing to revise our judgment.
