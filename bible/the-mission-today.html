INTRODUCTION TO BOOK 1: 'PRESUPPOSITIONS'

Every interpretation of Scripture begins with a foundation — whether acknowledged or not. These presuppositions are the lenses through which we read the text, shaping what we see, what we miss, and what we conclude.

The world approaches the Bible with skepticism, higher criticism, or naturalistic assumptions. We, however, begin with a different set: The Bible is inspired, inerrant, and self-authenticating. It is not the original text in its original context that is on trial — we, the readers, are. As I point out in the book, presuppositions are baggage that we bring to our reading of the Bible. This volume explores how modern theology often inherits assumptions that obscure the Bible's original message. Presuppositions manifest as false assumptions that distort our understanding of Scripture and our beliefs. From mistranslated words to imported worldviews, when any of us pick up our Bible, we unknowingly read Scripture through lenses shaped by beloved Sunday School teachers or pastors, or even by family traditions. I mention this not to assign blame, but to highlight a reality. This volume returns to the core framework: creation, covenant, resurrection, kingdom, restoration, and eternity. It invites readers back into the Bible's unified narrative: God's original intent was to dwell with humanity on the earth, and it culminates in Christ's return to fulfill that plan. Jesus came once. He's coming again - to stay!
