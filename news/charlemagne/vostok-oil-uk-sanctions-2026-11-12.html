12 November 2026 · Energy trade and sanctions

UK sanctions Vostok Oil, Russia’s Arctic crude supplier to China

The UK designation adds asset and transport restrictions to a Russian Arctic export project whose crude has reached China. The effect on shipments remains unknown.

What changed

On 12 November 2026, the UK added LLC Vostok Oil to its Russia sanctions list (RUS3809). The measures include an asset freeze, trust-services restrictions, director disqualification and transport sanctions. The notice says ships owned, controlled, chartered or operated by the designated entity are prohibited from entering UK ports and may be subject to movement directions or detention. The UK cited the company’s activity in Russia’s energy sector as its reason for designation.

The Russia–China trade link

Rosneft announced its first project loading at the Arctic terminal in September. In October, Reuters reported that the Vladimir Monomakh delivered Vostok Oil crude to Yantai, China, citing vessel tracking by Vortexa and LSEG. The operator’s announcement establishes a loading milestone, and the tracking accounts document a China-bound delivery. Russia and China are BRICS members. The documented connection is a commercial shipment between them.

Why it matters

The designation is in force. Its effects on contracting, shipping services and cargo volumes require separate evidence. This UK measure changes access to UK ports for covered ships. The notice does not establish a closure of the Northern Sea Route or a halt to Chinese purchases. The project had already begun loading crude before the designation. The announcement gives no new production or construction milestone.

What to watch

Will the operator report changes to liftings or service providers? Will subsequent tracking confirm repeat deliveries to China? Can trade data distinguish a sanctions-related change from the seasonal shift in Arctic traffic?
