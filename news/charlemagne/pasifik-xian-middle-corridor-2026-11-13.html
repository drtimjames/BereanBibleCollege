Pasifik and Xi’an rail operator sign Middle Corridor freight agreement

A 13 November filing outlines plans for more China–Türkiye freight trains via the Caspian and the Baku–Tbilisi–Kars railway. New departures and investment remain unconfirmed.

What the companies agreed

On 13 November 2026, Turkish logistics company Pasifik Eurasia disclosed that it had signed a memorandum of understanding with Xi’an Free Trade Port Construction and Operation Co. The filing did not specify a signing date. It describes plans to develop block-train services between China and Türkiye on the Middle Corridor, with onward connections to Europe and other markets. The companies intend to increase frequency and capacity, develop new services and plan joint infrastructure investment.

What is already operating

Pasifik said on 11 November that it introduced a weekly Chongqing–Türkiye schedule in June, using Kazakhstan, a Caspian Sea crossing, Azerbaijan, Georgia and the Baku–Tbilisi–Kars railway. That service provides a documented link to Chinese freight. The new agreement concerns the Xi’an partnership. The filing identifies no funding amount, construction start, launch date or additional train already in operation under the memorandum.

Why it matters

Additional departures could give Chinese shippers another option for time-sensitive container freight to Türkiye and Europe. The route depends on transfers between rail and Caspian shipping. Its practical value will depend on schedule reliability, available slots and rates.

What to watch next

When will the partners open bookings or announce a funded project? What will the first train carry, and will published operating figures show more frequent service?
