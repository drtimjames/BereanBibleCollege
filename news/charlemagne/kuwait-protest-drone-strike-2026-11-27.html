Kuwait summons Iranian ambassador after drone strike kills two Italian technicians

Kuwait delivered a formal protest on 27 November after a strike killed two Italian civilian technicians and injured two others. Kuwait and Italy blamed Iran, which denied involvement.

What changed

Kuwait’s Foreign Ministry summoned Iranian Ambassador Mohammad Toutounji on 27 November and handed him a formal protest note. Kuwait said a drone attack the day before killed two Italians, injured a Kuwaiti and a Pakistani, and caused significant damage. Italy’s foreign ministry confirmed that the two Italians worked for Rheinmetall Italia and were maintaining equipment for Kuwait’s air defenses.

Attribution remains disputed

Kuwait’s Foreign Ministry and Italian Foreign Minister Antonio Tajani attributed the attack to Iran. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi denied involvement and offered to investigate jointly, according to Reuters and Euronews. The offer alone does not establish that a joint inquiry is underway. The statements reviewed did not provide independently verifiable findings identifying who launched the drones.

Why it matters and what to watch

The deaths bring Italy directly into the diplomatic dispute over the strike and put the safety of civilian contractors supporting Gulf defense systems in focus. The statements reviewed did not announce a shipping restriction, interruption to Kuwaiti oil exports, or change to Hormuz access. Watch for Kuwait or Italy to publish attribution evidence, for an agreement on an inquiry, and for any separate notice from a port or terminal operator.
