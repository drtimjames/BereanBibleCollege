FAA lifts nationwide ground stop after communications outage

The FAA cleared departures to resume on 13 November. Airlines still faced delays and cancellations as they worked through the backlog.

Departures resume · 13 November 2026

The FAA said departures could resume after lifting the nationwide ground stop, the Associated Press reported. Reuters confirmed the stop was lifted and reported that the disruption lasted more than four hours. Airlines continued working through delays and cancellations.

What happened

On 13 November, the FAA ordered a nationwide departure halt for safety. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said the communications problem affected the 22 regional centers that direct aircraft between airports. The FAA advisory said inbound international flights would be handled case by case. KABC reported that some international flights later departed Los Angeles, while domestic flights to the contiguous United States were still being held there. Those departures preceded the nationwide restart.

What remains uncertain

Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said officials had no indication of a deliberate attack. Officials identified a telecommunications outage. The underlying fault remained under investigation.

Why it matters and what to watch

The disruption affected passenger and cargo departures across the network. Watch for the pace of schedule recovery and the FAA’s explanation of the communications failure.
