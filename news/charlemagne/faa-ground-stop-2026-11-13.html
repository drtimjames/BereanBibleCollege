FAA lifts nationwide ground stop after communications outage

The FAA said traffic resumed at 8:15 p.m. Eastern on 13 November and counted about 12,462 cancellations that day across all causes. The root cause of the communications failure remains under investigation.

What changed · 17 November 2026, 3:58 p.m. Pacific

On 17 November, Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said the cause was still undetermined. FAA Administrator Bryan Bedford called speculation of AI involvement unfounded, Reuters reported. This is an official assessment; a final technical finding remains outstanding.

Reuters, reporting the officials’ statements · 17 November

FAA reports the scale of cancellations and identifies two possible sources · 15 November 2026

Source detail added · 18 November 2026, 7:48 p.m. Pacific. The site names and precaution below come from the FAA’s 15 November account. Its final root-cause finding is still pending.

In its 15 November update, the FAA said operators canceled about 12,462 flights on 13 November, including 9,082 by major airlines. The FAA said the daily total included cancellations beyond those directly caused by the nationwide ground stop.

The FAA identified the Amarillo, Texas, radar site and the El Dorado, Arkansas, radio communications site as possible sources of the data disruption. Its network provider, L3Harris, had installed a new router at El Dorado. The FAA also said it disabled the same functionality on more than 60 other Juniper routers as a precaution. The agency said the network stabilized after both sites were isolated and that traffic resumed at 8:15 p.m. Eastern on 13 November. The FAA said it had seen no evidence of a cyberattack. The agency and its network provider continue to investigate the root cause.

FAA questions and answers · 15 November

Departures resume · 13 November 2026

The FAA said departures could resume after lifting the nationwide ground stop, the Associated Press reported. Reuters confirmed the stop was lifted and reported that the disruption lasted more than four hours. Airlines continued working through delays and cancellations.

What happened

On 13 November, the FAA ordered a nationwide departure halt for safety. The FAA said instability began at 17 of its 22 regional air traffic control centers and spread to several terminal radar approach control facilities. The FAA advisory said inbound international flights would be handled case by case. KABC reported that some international flights later departed Los Angeles, while domestic flights to the contiguous United States were still being held there. Those departures preceded the nationwide restart.

FAA returns to full operations · 14 November 2026

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said the FAA had returned to full operations. At his 14 November briefing, he attributed the outage to a router installed during work to replace a copper telecommunications line with fiber at a remote FAA facility. He said traffic from the router overwhelmed the network. Airlines were still working through delays and cancellations. The FAA’s 15 November account identified two possible sources and described the root cause as unresolved.

What officials said on 13 November

Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said officials had no indication of a deliberate attack. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said switches and routers in the FAA’s legacy communications network became overloaded, causing intermittent voice and radar disruptions. The Los Angeles Times reported that Duffy identified the affected system as the legacy time-division multiplexing network and said FAA fixes reduced the overload. Duffy said newly upgraded equipment was uninvolved. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin separately suggested to reporters that equipment installation had overwhelmed the older system, according to AP. At the time of the 13 November briefing, officials had yet to identify the specific trigger publicly.

Airline schedules still disrupted · 15 November 2026

Reuters reported that FlightAware had counted more than 1,600 U.S. flight cancellations and nearly 3,600 delays by the time of its Sunday report. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said he expected operations to be fully back to normal on Monday, 16 November. The Sunday figures were a snapshot of airline schedules after the restart.

Reuters on airline recovery · 15 November

Airline schedules recover · 16 November 2026

U.S. flight disruptions eased on Monday, according to The Washington Post, citing FlightAware data. American Airlines said late Sunday that it expected to operate a largely normal schedule on Monday. Some delays and cancellations continued, and the FAA’s root-cause investigation remained open.

American Airlines operations update · The Washington Post, 16 November

Senators request a pause in router rollout · 17 November 2026

Democratic members of the Senate Commerce Committee, led by Sen. Maria Cantwell, asked the FAA and Department of Transportation for evidence on the ground stop’s root cause and testing of the new equipment. They requested a pause on deploying additional Juniper routers pending testing. The letter is an oversight request. Later on 17 November, the FAA said it was immediately pausing deployment of additional Juniper routers pending testing, Reuters reported. Officials have yet to publish a final cause finding. Watch for the testing criteria and any restart decision.

Why it matters and what to watch

The disruption affected passenger and cargo departures across the network. Watch for the pace of schedule recovery and a final technical finding on the network failure.
