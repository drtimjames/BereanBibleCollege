Saudi Arabia moves ahead of UAE in India’s October oil imports, Reuters data show

Cargo period: October 2026 · Reuters reporting: 19 November · Published 19 November 2026, 5:17 a.m. Pacific

What changed. Reuters puts October deliveries to India at about 790,000 barrels a day from Saudi Arabia and 612,000 from the UAE. Russia remained India’s largest supplier. The figures rank origins and leave port-by-port routing open. Reuters put Russian deliveries at about 1.85 million barrels a day and India’s total at about 5.58 million barrels a day.

Source comparison. India’s Directorate General of Maritime Administration cited a separate later estimate of 5.54 million barrels a day delivered for use by Indian refiners. Its preliminary supplier table also put Saudi Arabia ahead of the UAE, with different estimates. The administration cautions that counts including offshore transfers can capture cargo ultimately sent to other markets. These datasets should be read on their own terms.

Why it matters. Russia remained the top source for India, and the two Gulf suppliers’ relative positions changed in the month. Reuters separately reported stronger Saudi exports from Yanbu, an outlet served by the Saudi East–West pipeline. That provides context for possible alternatives to Hormuz. The India import totals do not establish the share loaded at Yanbu or changes to access through either Hormuz or Bab el-Mandeb.

Next evidence. Watch for November imports, loading ports by origin, final discharge records and consistent measures of bypass throughput.

What changed · 19 November 2026, 4:16 a.m. Pacific. China published the declaration after the three-way meeting. Reuters reported that the Kremlin also released it. The text says the three countries plan to increase production and supply of refined petroleum products and emphasizes stable energy flows. It also calls for dialogue to reduce nuclear risks and prevent proliferation. The fuel pledge sets no volumes, deadlines, shipment routes or implementing measures. Look for refinery or export commitments, evidence of actual shipments and a dated maritime notice before treating the declaration as an operational change.

Gateway Distriparks wins eight-year rail yard contract at India’s Nhava Sheva port

Company disclosure: 18 November 2026 India time · Published 19 November 2026, 2:43 a.m. Pacific

The handover date and any throughput gains remain unconfirmed. Kpler estimates Hormuz crude flows at 8.5 million barrels a day

Preliminary figures through 16 November 2026 · AP reporting 18 November · Published 18 November 2026, 8:46 p.m. Pacific

What changed. Preliminary Kpler figures reported by AP put crude flows through Hormuz at 8.5 million barrels a day and Middle East crude flows across all routes at about 15 million a day. The two estimates cover different routes. AP reported that Kpler said the preliminary strait figure could rise as more data came in. The all-routes estimate includes shipments via pipelines that bypass the strait. It covers crude and does not measure LNG or all vessel traffic.

Why it matters. The preliminary estimates show how much the assessment changes when flows outside Hormuz are included. The U.S. Energy Information Administration lists Saudi and Emirati bypass pipelines and identifies China and India among the largest historical destinations for crude and condensate transiting Hormuz. That establishes exposure for major buyers in both countries. The new figures do not give buyer-specific deliveries or prove how much capacity remains available at each bypass.

Uncertainty and next evidence. Kpler’s crude analysis chief told Rudaw on 16 November that alternatives were carrying a larger share of regional exports than before the war. His interview used a broader regional estimate. Watch for a revised, like-for-like series, destination-level arrivals and operator updates on throughput at the bypass export terminals. The preliminary traffic figures predate the 18 November U.S. blockade guidance on Iranian cargo transfers and do not measure its effect.

U.S. warns ships receiving Iranian cargo at sea may face blockade enforcement

Advisory and reporting: 18 November 2026 · Published 18 November 2026, 5:43 p.m. Pacific

What changed. The U.S. Navy says ships receiving cargo loaded in Iran from a vessel subject to the blockade may face enforcement even if they never called at an Iranian port. U.S. Naval Forces Central Command set out the policy in an addendum circulated by the Joint Maritime Information Center on 18 November. The notice lists possible boarding, diversion or capture under the blockade the U.S. reimposed on 14 July.

Scope. The Navy says neutral Hormuz transit to or from non-Iranian destinations remains permitted under the blockade, subject to possible inspection. It also details a vetting process for humanitarian cargo headed to Iran. The advisory establishes how the U.S. may act. It identifies no first enforcement case under the new guidance and gives no estimate of its effect on traffic.

BRICS trade connection. Iran is the cargo origin specified by the notice. Reuters reported on 16 October that shipping analysts tracked some Iranian LPG to onward transfers off Malaysia, with China assessed as the likely destination for much of it. That assessment describes prior trade, and the current consignee or destination of any individual cargo would need separate confirmation.

Why it matters. Vessel operators and cargo buyers have to establish where transferred cargo was loaded as well as where the receiving ship called. The near-term question is whether the advisory changes chartering and insurance decisions or leads to a documented boarding or diversion involving a receiving vessel. Watch for the first publicly confirmed case and independent evidence of changed export volumes.

All 19 rescued from Egypt–Romania LPG carrier Gas Husky off Bulgaria

Incident and source reports: 17 November 2026 · Published 17 November 2026, 4:02 p.m. Pacific

Confirmed rescue. Bulgaria’s transport ministry says all 19 Gas Husky crew members were evacuated from a liferaft and flown to the Chaika air base in Varna. The ministry ended the search-and-rescue operation at 13:15 local time on 17 November. Its initial notice recorded the emergency beacon at 08:02, approximately 50 nautical miles east of Byala, and described an overturned vessel believed to be Gas Husky.

18 November, 12:09 a.m. Pacific update. Bulgaria’s coastal warning 854/26 described Gas Husky adrift and listing at 10:50 UTC on 17 November. Final vessel status remains unresolved. The warning, still listed in the 18 November bulletin, identifies a local navigation hazard. AP and Reuters separately attributed reports of a sinking to Bulgaria’s defence ministry. The latest vessel condition, salvage and pollution status remain open. Original Bulgarian maritime bulletin, NAVWARN 854/26 · Archived warning text · Associated Press account

What changed · 18 November 2026, 7:35 a.m. Pacific. In a Form 6-K, owner StealthGas reported that Gas Husky was struck on 17 November and confirmed that all 19 crew were brought safely ashore. It said the ship’s condition remained unknown and confirmed Alexandria as the loading port and Midia as the intended discharge port. The filing puts the owner’s account on record. It leaves the cause, responsibility and any salvage or pollution status open. StealthGas Form 6-K · 18 November

What changed · 18 November 2026, 6:25 p.m. Pacific. Philippine Migrant Workers Secretary Hans Cacdac said two of the 19 rescued Filipino crew members sustained minor injuries and that his department was arranging their return, subject to Bulgarian requirements. Speaking to GMA News on 19 November Philippine time, Cacdac said all 19 were accounted for and staying at a hotel in Bulgaria. Watch for confirmation of their return and separate findings on the vessel and cargo. Secretary Cacdac’s interview with GMA News · 19 November Philippine time

Cargo connection. The ministry identified the Liberian-flagged ship’s cargo as 3,400 tonnes of propane-butane moving from Egypt to Romania. Maritime.bg directly reported ship manager Stealth Maritime’s statement that the cargo loaded at Alexandria was destined for Midia. This establishes a specific Egypt-linked energy shipment; Egypt is a BRICS member.

Attributed strike account. The manager said the vessel was struck in Bulgaria’s exclusive economic zone, Maritime.bg reported. The master described suspected drone impacts. The original Bulgarian rescue notices reviewed here do not establish the mechanism or identify a responsible party. Those claims remain distinct from the confirmed rescue and cargo account.

Why it matters · Day Watch analysis. This casualty interrupts a named LPG voyage on the western Black Sea route. It follows the separately reported Betul 8 wheat casualty. Their proximity alone does not establish a common attacker, coordinated campaign or a wider shipping closure.

Next evidence. Seek the maritime investigation, the operator’s final vessel-condition report, cargo and pollution assessments, crew medical updates and any dated navigation restriction. No verified regional access restriction or quantified wider trade loss is established.

Passenger operator Grand Service Express reported delays on trains to occupied Crimea. Freight capacity, road repairs and maritime passage remain unresolved. Its announcement and the operator notice establish a fresh interruption after the passenger resumption on 17 November. The bridge serves a Russian transport route to occupied Crimea. Earlier passenger resumption · 17 November

Events and source reports: 16–17 November 2026 · Published 17 November 2026, 4:02 p.m. Pacific

What changed. An overnight suspension was followed by documented passenger-train movements. Treat the reopening as a specific recovery milestone; road access, freight capacity and navigation through the strait require their own evidence.

Confirmed official accounts. Russia’s transport ministry said road and rail traffic were temporarily suspended after another reported drone attack overnight on 17 November. Its statement is reproduced on the Russian government’s public-information site. Later that day, train operator Grand Service Express said rail traffic had reopened. Damage and attribution. Ukraine claimed the strike and described collapsed road sections and damage to the railway arch, the Associated Press reported. The extent and consequences of that damage remain disputed. The operator’s completed train movements establish a narrower fact than either an intact crossing or a permanently severed rail link. Disruption can alter the availability and timing of freight movements, but these sources do not quantify lost cargo, international trade volumes or destination markets. Next evidence. What road access has been restored? Which freight trains are moving, at what capacity? Is there a dated port or navigation authority notice restricting the channel? Distinguish repair planning and announced construction from completed repairs and operating capacity.

Russia–China container freight shifts to Pacific rail links after Black Sea pullback

Initial rail arrivals 1, 9 and 10 November 2026 · Operator reports 2 and 12 November · Traffic reporting 17 November 2026

Rail services have begun. FESCO said two trains from Novorossiysk delivered 304 twenty-foot equivalent units of agricultural cargo to Vladivostok on 9 and 10 November. It plans to move the containers onward by sea to China and run up to two trains a month. Delo Group said a separate Ruscon train carrying about 2,000 tonnes of flax reached Nakhodka on 1 November for onward shipment to Tianjin. The operators confirmed the Russian rail arrivals. Delivery in China for these consignments and sustained frequencies remain unverified.

Why it matters. On 17 November, The Moscow Times reported that container carriers had withdrawn from Novorossiysk by October and that traffic shifted toward Russia’s Baltic and Far Eastern ports, citing a shipping executive and Gazprombank’s forecasting center. The routes documented by FESCO and Delo give Russian exporters an alternative for China-bound containers. The figures concern container traffic and provide no basis for inferring a closure to tankers or to all commercial shipping.

What to watch. Will the consignments reach their Chinese ports, and how much volume can the new rail services sustain? Will the planned westbound FESCO service start, and will Pacific terminal or rail capacity constrain bookings?

Maersk and Hapag-Lloyd schedule three China–Europe services to return via Suez

Carrier announcement and Strategic Watch reporting · 16 November 2026

Confirmed decision. Maersk and Hapag-Lloyd announced that three joint Gemini services will switch from sailing around the Cape of Good Hope to the Red Sea and Suez Canal in both directions. The first westbound voyages are due to berth at their Chinese loading ports on 4 December 2026 (Mary Maersk, Ningbo), 20 December 2026 (Alette Maersk, Shanghai) and 14 January 2027 (Damietta Express, Shanghai). These are estimated port calls. The advisories give no confirmed canal-crossing dates for these voyages.

Why it matters. The published rotations connect Chinese export ports with northwestern Europe via Tanjung Pelepas in Malaysia and Egypt’s Suez Canal. China and Egypt are BRICS members, and Malaysia is a partner country. The routing decision was made by the carriers. They expect shorter transit times, and Maersk says further changes depend on conditions in the Red Sea.

What to watch. Do the three ships depart on the revised rotations and complete their Red Sea and Suez passages? Will regular sailings follow, and will the change shorten actual delivery times? The impact on cargo volumes and on other carriers’ Cape routings remains open.

Sabetta tightens ice access at Russia’s Yamal LNG export port

Port captain’s order 11 November 2026 · Port bulletin 12 November 2026 · Strategic Watch update 16 November 2026

Confirmed access restriction. The Western Arctic ports administration’s 12 November ice bulletin records tighter access in Sabetta harbor and its approaches under order 15/2026. Ice2 vessels require icebreaker escort. The bulletin permits Ice3 vessels and higher to navigate independently or with an escort. Ice1 vessels, vessels without ice reinforcement, and tug-and-barge combinations are barred from navigating in ice under the order.

Why it matters. Sabetta loads LNG from Russia’s Yamal project. Yamal LNG signed a 20-year supply contract with China National Petroleum Corporation for three million tonnes a year. The EU energy regulator identifies Sabetta as the loading port and China as the contract destination. Tighter ice rules can narrow the pool of eligible ships and increase reliance on escorts. Their effect on LNG sailings or delivery volumes remains unverified.

What to watch. Do escort waiting times rise, do LNG loading schedules change, and does the port captain revise the thresholds as ice conditions develop?

It reported no attacks on shipping in the Red Sea or Bab el-Mandeb since 24 August and noted that MSCIO logged a 6 November explosion as suspicious activity. This is a separate comparison from the transit counts above, and the explosion’s cause remains unverified. UKMTO weekly overview, 13 November.

The Suez Canal Authority’s notice documents EVER EON’s individual passage and describes further Evergreen voyages as planned. Will subsequent carrier schedules and tracking show regular end-to-end Red Sea service?
