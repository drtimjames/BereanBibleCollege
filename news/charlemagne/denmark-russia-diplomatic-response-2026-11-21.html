Denmark expels Russian defense attaché and seeks tighter EU travel rules

Denmark announced the expulsion after its security service attributed a thwarted September assassination attempt to Russian intelligence. EU-wide travel measures remain proposals.

What changed

Denmark announced on 20 November that it was expelling Russia’s defense attaché. On 21 November, Reuters reported that a Danish note circulated to EU governments proposed ending tourist visas for Russian citizens and ceasing recognition of Russian non-biometric passports across the Schengen area. Reuters said two diplomats confirmed the note. The report establishes a Danish proposal. It identifies no EU-wide decision putting those measures into force.

What officials established

The Danish Security and Intelligence Service, PET, said it and police thwarted an alleged assassination attempt against a Ukrainian-American citizen on 25 September. It attributed planning to Russian intelligence as an intelligence assessment. PET said two people had been charged. The charges are allegations, and individual criminal liability is for the courts. The Russian embassy rejected the attribution, Reuters reported. PET said its assessment concerned a specific target.

Why it matters and what to watch

Denmark’s response moved from a national diplomatic action to a request for coordinated European travel policy. Any change for travelers depends on what the relevant governments adopt and when they put it into effect. Look for a published EU or Schengen decision, its effective date and exceptions, and any findings disclosed in the Danish criminal proceedings.
