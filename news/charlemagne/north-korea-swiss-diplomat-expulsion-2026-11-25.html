North Korea orders Swiss diplomat to leave and demands office closure

Pyongyang gave the head of the Swiss office 48 hours to leave, citing sanctions on a North Korean camp that the EU says received Ukrainian children brought from Russian-occupied territory.

What changed

North Korea’s Foreign Ministry declared the head of the Swiss diplomatic office, Sylvain Astier, unwelcome and ordered him to depart within 48 hours, Reuters and AP reported, citing North Korea’s state news agency. Pyongyang also demanded closure of the office and said it was responding to Swiss sanctions on the Songdowon International Children’s Camp. In comments to Reuters on 24 November, the Swiss foreign ministry said its sanctions policy was independent of the office reopening. Switzerland’s response to the closure order and the order’s implementation remain to be confirmed.

Why it matters

Switzerland says it reopened the office on 19 October to restore direct contact and explore whether humanitarian programmes could resume. The office reports to the Swiss Embassy in Beijing. Pyongyang’s order threatens the local channel opened in October. The EU separately listed the camp in September, alleging that it hosted Ukrainian children taken from Russian-occupied territory and contributed to their forced assimilation. That is the EU’s stated basis for the listing.

What to watch

Confidence is high that North Korea announced the order. Look for Swiss confirmation of staff departure or closure and any indication that contacts and humanitarian discussions can continue through Beijing.
