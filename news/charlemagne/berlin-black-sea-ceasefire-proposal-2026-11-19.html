Meeting and statement · 19 November 2026 · Published 19 November 2026, 11:38 a.m. Pacific

Berlin leaders call for a Black Sea food-export ceasefire and a winter energy truce

What changed. Germany, France, the United Kingdom and Ukraine called for a winter energy truce and a ceasefire to protect Black Sea food exports. The proposal has produced no ceasefire or change to navigation rules. The published statement is attributed to the leaders of those four countries. It records U.S. participation in the Berlin meeting.

What the statement proposes

The leaders asked Russia to agree to a winter pause in attacks on energy infrastructure. They separately called for a ceasefire that would allow food exports to move through the Black Sea. The statement also calls for further pressure on Russian oil and gas revenue and continued action against vessels used to circumvent sanctions. It specifies no new vessel designations or enforcement date.

The leaders reaffirmed earlier work on security guarantees that would take effect after a ceasefire. Possible measures include U.S.-led verification and a European-led multinational force with a role at sea, on land and in the air. The statement describes options that would require further decisions. It issues no deployment order.

What Moscow has said

Earlier on 19 November, Russian President Vladimir Putin said Moscow would no longer consider a moratorium on strikes at sea and on energy infrastructure after the Kerch Bridge attacks, Reuters reported. Those remarks preceded the written Berlin statement and concerned an earlier proposal. A specific Russian response to the Berlin text remains to be established.

Why it matters for trade

The proposed food-export ceasefire concerns a route used by BRICS members as trading partners. Bulgaria’s transport ministry recently documented wheat moving from Romania to Egypt aboard the Betul 8. A truce could reduce risk for voyages like that one if its parties agree how to observe and enforce it. The Berlin publication itself changes no shipping restrictions, insurance terms or port procedures.

What to watch

Watch for Moscow’s formal response, a text accepted by both sides, defined coverage for ports and ships, and any subsequent notice from maritime authorities or changes in operator schedules.
