Solomon Islands signs treaty with Australia barring foreign military bases

The signed treaty names Australia as the primary security partner and covers ports and other sensitive infrastructure. It takes effect after both governments complete their domestic procedures and exchange notices.

What changed

Australian Prime Minister Anthony Albanese and Solomon Islands Prime Minister Matthew Wale signed the bilateral treaty on 25 November. Article 5 bars foreign military or security bases and commits Solomon Islands to protect sensitive infrastructure from militarisation, interference and unauthorised access. The treaty defines sensitive infrastructure to include ports, airports, energy, digital and telecommunications facilities. Under Article 9, the agreement enters into force after the later of the two written notifications that domestic requirements are complete.

Why it matters

The agreement sets rules for foreign security activity and sensitive infrastructure in the South Pacific. It names Australia as Solomon Islands’ primary security partner and provides for closer police cooperation. Solomon Islands also signed a security agreement with China in 2022. Wale declined to confirm whether that agreement would be renewed, AP reported. The new treaty commits the parties on security and infrastructure. The signing announcement identifies no change to merchant port access.

What to watch

Confidence is high in the signed text. The immediate questions are when both notifications are exchanged and how Solomon Islands implements the infrastructure commitments alongside its existing external agreements.
