The Day Watch Morning Brief

Ukraine’s power disruptions, proposed U.S. tariffs, a Pacific security treaty and Optimizely’s outside review.

What changed on the ground, and what still depends on a later decision or finding?

Ukraine reports energy shutdowns and backup power at Zaporizhzhia

On 25 November, DTEK said Russian strikes shut down one of its thermal plants. Naftogaz suspended a gas production site in Poltava region after a fire. Ukrainian officials reported outages in five regions. Energoatom said emergency generators supplied the Zaporizhzhia nuclear plant after it lost off-site power. The IAEA was investigating the disconnection. Watch for confirmed grid restoration and operator restart timelines.

U.S. proposes new tariffs for 14 economies

On 25 November, the U.S. Trade Representative proposed new Section 301 duties after finding that industrial excess-capacity practices in 14 economies burden U.S. commerce. The proposed rates depend on the product, the economy and existing duties. China and India are among the economies covered. The proposals set no collection date. Comments are due 5 January 2027, with hearings scheduled to start 25 January.

USTR findings and proposals · China notice and annexes · Full report

Solomon Islands signs treaty with Australia barring foreign bases

The leaders signed the agreement on 25 November in Canberra. It names Australia as Solomon Islands’ primary security partner, bars foreign military or security bases and commits Solomon Islands to protect sensitive infrastructure, including ports. It takes effect after both governments complete their domestic procedures and exchange notices. Prime Minister Matthew Wale declined to say whether the separate 2022 agreement with China would be renewed.

Optimizely commissions an independent review of the website-code incident

On 25 November, Optimizely said Mandiant is investigating the 16 November Web Experimentation incident. The vendor says affected projects were cleaned that day and that it is assessing possible effects on people who used its own login page. Its public update gives no tally of confirmed card theft and says it has received no reports of infected devices. Watch for the independent findings and site-specific notices.
