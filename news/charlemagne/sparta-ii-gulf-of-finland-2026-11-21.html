Finland sets safety zone around damaged Kaliningrad-bound cargo ship

Finland lifted wider temporary air and sea restrictions in the eastern Gulf of Finland on 21 November. Its Border Guard separately established a local safety zone around the burning Russian cargo ship Sparta II after the ship drifted into Finnish waters.

What authorities confirmed

Finnish and Russian authorities reported a drone strike on the cargo ship in international waters on 21 November. Reuters reported that the Russian-flagged ship had left St. Petersburg for Kaliningrad. Finnish Defence Forces lifted the wider restrictions at 2 p.m. Finnish time. At 3:03 p.m., the Finnish Border Guard said rescue units were at the ship off Loviisa, the crew had not had to abandon it, and responders had observed no environmental damage. The agency reported no danger to other maritime traffic and said it was coordinating with neighboring countries, including Russian maritime rescue authorities.

What changed · 22 November 2026, 8:03 a.m. Pacific

On 22 November, the Finnish Border Guard said it was still leading firefighting and cooling with Estonian and Russian assistance. Responders were managing the risk that water accumulating aboard could destabilize the ship. In a later briefing carried by Yle, the agency said tow preparations were under way. Reuters reported, citing Finnish authorities, that visible flames had been extinguished while hot spots remained, and that all 16 crew had evacuated on 21 November, with one slightly injured.

The tow and handover were still unconfirmed in those accounts. The Border Guard reported no known danger to surrounding maritime traffic in its morning statement. The cargo remains unconfirmed. For the St. Petersburg–Kaliningrad route, the next evidence is a completed tow, the official status of the local restriction and any operator notice affecting other voyages.

Finnish Border Guard statement · 22 November · Finnish Border Guard briefing via Yle · Reuters · 22 November

What changed · 21 November 2026, 12:10 p.m. Pacific

In a statement issued at 8:42 p.m. Finnish time on 21 November, the Finnish Border Guard confirmed that the crew escaped in lifeboats and transferred to a nearby cargo ship. Reuters, citing a Border Guard commander’s interview with Yle, put the crew at 16 and reported that they were headed back to Russia.

The Sparta II remained at anchor off Loviisa. Responders from Finland, Estonia and Russia were assisting, and firefighting continued. The local restriction remained in place. The Border Guard reported no known danger to surrounding traffic and said responders planned to prepare the ship for a tow into Russia’s area of responsibility. The tow had yet to occur in that account. The agency said its next statement would come on 22 November.

With the crew off the vessel, the next decisions concern firefighting, salvage and access around the ship. Watch for confirmation of the tow and the lifting of the local restriction.

Finnish Border Guard statement · 21 November · Reuters follow-up · 21 November

What changed · 21 November 2026, 11:09 a.m. Pacific

Finland’s coast guard commander said the ship had anchored and evacuation was under way. Estonia’s navy commander subsequently told Sky News the crew was aboard. A completed evacuation and the proposed tow remain unconfirmed. Finnish authorities said Russian rescue vessels were expected to assist and planned to tow the ship into Russian waters, Verkkouutiset reported from the briefing. The local safety zone was still in place in that account.

Finnish authorities’ press briefing via Yle · Verkkouutiset on the rescue and proposed tow · Sky News, quoting Estonia’s navy commander

Attribution and cargo

A Finnish military spokesperson assessed that Ukraine was likely responsible, Reuters reported. Responsibility remains unconfirmed. The Finnish Border Guard told Reuters it had no confirmed information about the cargo. Reuters reported that the vessel is under U.S. sanctions and linked to Oboronlogistika, a provider of logistics services to Russia’s defence ministry. Kaliningrad’s governor said regional supplies were unaffected by the incident.

Why it matters

The ship was on a Russian domestic Baltic cargo route connecting the mainland to its Kaliningrad exclave. The wider restriction was temporary, and the later safety zone was vessel-specific. The incident therefore establishes a disruption to one voyage and a localized restriction. It leaves the effect on other sailings or regional supplies unresolved.

What to watch

Has the fire been extinguished and the crew accounted for? Will the ship need a tow, and when will Finland lift the local safety zone? Do operator schedules show cancellations on the Kaliningrad route? What evidence will investigators release on attribution?
