South Korea protests Russian military flights near Ulleung Island after brief shelter alert

South Korea’s military said Russian aircraft entered its air defence identification zone but stayed outside territorial airspace. Seoul summoned Russia’s military attaché after correcting an earlier alert.

What changed

South Korea’s Ministry of National Defense protested after Russian military aircraft entered the country’s air defence identification zone and approached Ulleung Island on 24 November. The ministry summoned Russia’s military attaché and called for steps to prevent a recurrence, Yonhap reported. South Korea’s Joint Chiefs of Staff said the aircraft stayed outside territorial airspace.

The public warning and correction

The incident prompted a brief shelter alert for island residents. An initial central-government message described an airspace breach. A provincial notice misidentified the aircraft as possibly North Korean. The military clarified the aircraft were Russian and that the entry was into the identification zone. An identification zone extends beyond sovereign airspace.

Why it matters

South Korea’s account establishes an approach and a public warning. Moscow disputed any violation of international law and said it did not recognize obligations arising from the zone, according to a Russian Embassy statement carried by TASS and reported by Yonhap. The available statements leave the purpose of the flight unresolved. For residents, the conflicting messages make the sequence and accuracy of future alerts a practical concern.

What to watch

Will Seoul release a review of the mistaken alerts or agree with Moscow on measures to reduce repeat incidents?
