Maritime · Published 13 November 2026

Lower Mississippi reopens with restrictions after tanker collision

The Coast Guard lifted the full closure on the evening of 13 November, with one-way traffic and a safety zone remaining near the New Orleans collision site. On 15 November, an NTSB official said the tanker lost steering. Investigators are still determining why. Everyone aboard survived.

What changed · 18 November 2026, 1:17 p.m. Pacific

A Coast Guard rule published on 18 November sets the salvage safety zone through 11 December unless the Captain of the Port cancels it earlier. It covers waters within 500 feet of the left descending bank between river miles 95.5 and 96.5. Entry requires Coast Guard authorization. The agency expects salvage to take several weeks. The rule governs the marked salvage area. Mariners should check Vessel Traffic Service notices for current channel restrictions. Watch for an early cancellation or a revised salvage timetable.

Coast Guard temporary final rule · 18 November

NTSB identifies steering failure · 15 November 2026

An NTSB official told Reuters the Atlantic Asphalt suffered a steering failure. Investigators have yet to determine what caused the steering to fail. The NTSB lists the investigation as ongoing. Its case summary records 190 people aboard the riverboat (140 passengers and 50 crew), of whom 36 were hospitalized following the collision. These are the NTSB’s cumulative figures. The city separately reported that four people remained hospitalized on 16 November. Watch for its preliminary report and for Coast Guard notices on salvage and river access.

NTSB investigation DCA27MM003 · Reuters report on the NTSB update · 15 November

River reopens with restrictions · 13 November 2026

A Vessel Traffic Service notice issued at 6:35 p.m. local time on 13 November lifted the full closure. The Coast Guard’s 7 p.m. bulletin kept one-way traffic between mile markers 95.5 and 96.5, required vessels to travel at their slowest safe speed and coordinate with Vessel Traffic Service, and established a 500-foot safety zone extending from the left descending bank. Axios reported the overturned cruise boat remained within the safety zone and removal could take weeks. Reuters reported that NTSB investigators recovered the tanker’s voyage data recorder. The investigation has yet to establish the cause. Watch for changes to the traffic restrictions and the NTSB’s preliminary findings.

Vessel Traffic Service reopening notice, 13 November · Coast Guard Marine Safety Information Bulletin 033, 13 November · Reuters, reporting on the NTSB investigation

Four remain hospitalized · 16 November 2026

New Orleans officials said four people remained hospitalized on Monday, including one in serious condition, Axios reported. The Coast Guard was still leading salvage. In its 15 November notice, the city said one-way traffic and a safety zone were in effect and vessels needed to coordinate with Vessel Traffic Service.

Axios, citing city officials on 16 November · City of New Orleans river traffic and response update, 15 November

Six remain hospitalized · 15 November 2026

The City of New Orleans said six people remained hospitalized on Sunday, down from eight on Saturday, according to the Associated Press. Thirty-two people were taken to hospitals after the collision.

Associated Press, citing the city’s 15 November statement

Eight remain hospitalized · 14 November 2026

Mayor Helena Moreno said eight of the 32 people taken to hospitals after the collision remained hospitalized on Saturday. Two were in serious condition, according to local reporting on the city update.

WBRZ report on the mayor’s update

Port files federal suit · 14 November 2026

The Port of New Orleans sued over damage to its terminal following the 13 November collision, alleging at least $1 million in damage. The Associated Press reported that a federal judge authorized the tanker’s seizure. The port’s allegations are pending and do not establish the collision’s cause.

Associated Press reporting on the suit and order · WDSU reporting from court records

Tanker status · 14 November 2026

Ship manager OSM Thome said the Atlantic Asphalt had separated from the cruise boat and was stable at anchor while damage was assessed. The company said all tanker crew were safe and accounted for. The statement gave no Coast Guard reopening time or finding on the cause.

OSM Thome statement, 14 November

Rescue update · 13 November 2026

Mayor Helena Moreno said everyone aboard was accounted for, with no fatalities. At a briefing reported by WVUE, Moreno said 32 people had been taken to hospitals and none was in critical condition at the time. WVUE also reported that the National Transportation Safety Board dispatched an investigative team. The cause remains under investigation.

WVUE coverage of the mayor’s briefing and investigation, 13 November

What happened

On 13 November, the asphalt tanker Atlantic Asphalt struck the American Heritage, an American Cruise Lines paddlewheel boat carrying 129 passengers. The cruise boat rolled onto its side and was partially submerged. Emergency crews and nearby vessels assisted in the rescue.

What remains uncertain

The cruise line said it was working with the Coast Guard. U.S. Coast Guard Capt. Melanie Burnham said the tanker appeared to have lost steering, the Associated Press reported. Investigators had yet to establish the cause of the collision. The tanker’s cargo status remained unconfirmed in the reports reviewed.

Why it matters and what to watch

One-way traffic limits passage through part of the Lower Mississippi during salvage. Watch for Coast Guard notices changing those limits.
