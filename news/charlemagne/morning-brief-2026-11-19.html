The Day Watch Morning Brief

Delayed trains reach Kerch, Shenzhen leaders call for more fuel supply, and the European Commission reports progress restoring its applications.

How much of today’s news changes what can actually move, operate or be relied on?

Delayed passenger trains reach Kerch after one track reopens

On 19 November, Grand Service Express told Interfax that all three passenger trains delayed by the Kerch Bridge closure had reached Kerch in Russian-occupied Crimea. The arrivals show that passenger trains completed the crossing after Russian authorities reopened one track. The operator said it planned to resume scheduled departures. Freight capacity, the second rail track, road access and maritime passage still require separate confirmation.

Shenzhen declaration calls for more refined fuel supply

The United States, China and Russia called for increasing refined petroleum production and supply in their 19 November Shenzhen declaration. They also called for dialogue to reduce nuclear risks. The fuel pledge gives no volumes, deadlines or implementing measures and announces no new terms for passage through Hormuz. Refinery commitments, export schedules and maritime notices would show whether the declaration changes fuel supply or shipping access.

Shenzhen declaration · 19 November · AP on the summit · 19 November · Developing report

European Commission says most affected apps are back after cyber campaign

On 19 November the Commission said it detected the campaign on 22 October and confirmed it on 26 October. It took some applications offline as a precaution and says most have been restored. The Commission found no evidence of a breach affecting its staff as a whole. The investigation remains open, including questions about narrower exposure. Watch for a complete service inventory and any notices to affected people.

Commission briefing · 19 November · Full report

EU negotiators agree to postpone methane import requirements

The EU Council said on 19 November that negotiators provisionally agreed to shift selected fossil-fuel import monitoring requirements from January 2027 to January 2029 and methane-intensity reporting from August 2028 to August 2030. The change still requires formal adoption and gives importers more time to meet documentation requirements if enacted. It provides no new import authorization. Watch for formal votes, the final legal text and guidance for importers.

EU Council announcement · 19 November · Developing report

Saudi Arabia overtakes UAE in India’s October crude imports

On 19 November Reuters reported that India took about 790,000 barrels a day from Saudi Arabia in October and 612,000 from the UAE. Russia remained the largest supplier. India’s maritime administration published a separate preliminary estimate with the same Saudi–UAE ordering and different volumes. Supplier rankings establish where crude originated. November arrivals, loading ports and discharge records would show whether the shift persisted and how much used routes around Hormuz.

Indian maritime administration’s October report · Reuters · 19 November · Developing report
