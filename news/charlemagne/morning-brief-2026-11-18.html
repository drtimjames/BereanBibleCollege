The Day Watch Morning Brief

A Hormuz rescue, a disrupted Black Sea LPG shipment, and the scope of the Optimizely incident.

What can operators now confirm about the people, cargo and website visitors exposed to these three disruptions?

One crew member still missing after Hormuz tanker attack

On 18 November, ADNOC Logistics & Services reported one crew member missing and 11 injured after an attack on one of its vessels the previous evening. Its statement withheld the ship’s name. Reuters separately reported that Omani responders rescued 24 people from the LPG tanker Tara Gas and were still fighting its fire. India’s earlier count covered Indian nationals. The search outcome and any change in commercial transit remain the next tests.

ADNOC L&S statement · 18 November · Reuters, including Omani rescue figures · 18 November · Developing report

Gas Husky owner confirms 19 crew safe; vessel status unresolved

In a filing dated 18 November, StealthGas confirmed that its LPG carrier was struck on 17 November off Bulgaria and that all 19 crew reached shore safely. The owner identified Alexandria, Egypt, as the loading port and Midia, Romania, as the intended destination. It said the vessel’s condition was unknown. The filing documents the disruption of a specific Egypt–Romania energy shipment. Watch for an authoritative vessel-status, salvage or pollution update.

StealthGas Form 6-K · 18 November · Developing report and Bulgarian warnings

Optimizely says fewer than 5% of customers were affected

Optimizely’s 18 November update says malicious code was removed after a 16 November incident involving Web Experimentation. Report URI separately observed affected website code associated with 178 organizations. The vendor percentage and the researcher’s count measure different things. Website visitors could have been exposed to attempted payment or cryptocurrency theft. Visitor losses and customer-network compromise require separate evidence. Visa’s separately reported provider incident has no established connection.

Optimizely status update · 18 November · Full reporting and researcher documentation

Evening update · Hormuz and Iranian cargo

What changed · 18 November 2026, 5:43 p.m. Pacific. The U.S. Navy says ships receiving cargo loaded in Iran from a vessel subject to the blockade may face enforcement even if they never called at an Iranian port. This makes cargo origin a separate consideration from a vessel’s own route. The advisory says neutral passage to or from non-Iranian destinations remains permitted, subject to possible inspection. Separately, a navigation warning reported Tara Gas still burning and adrift off Oman at 15:00 UTC. The next tests are a documented enforcement case under the updated guidance and a rescue or salvage update on the tanker.
