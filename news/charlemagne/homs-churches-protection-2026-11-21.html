Syrian church leaders call for protection after Homs archbishop is attacked

Archbishop Jean-Abdo Arbach told Aid to the Church in Need he was safe. Three patriarchs called for investigations, protection and equal citizenship.

What happened

Melkite Greek Catholic Archbishop Jean-Abdo Arbach was attacked and robbed in his office in Homs, Syria, on 14 November, according to Aid to the Church in Need and Vatican News. Arbach told Aid to the Church in Need on 17 November that he was safe and that the investigation was continuing. The charity published his account the following day, and Vatican News reported on the case and the churches’ response on 20 November.

What the churches asked for

In a joint statement dated 17 November, the Greek Orthodox, Syriac Orthodox and Melkite Greek Catholic patriarchs called for Syrian authorities to investigate the attacks they cited, share their findings and protect citizens. They said Christians had faced recurring violence and reaffirmed peaceful relations with Muslim neighbors on the basis of equal citizenship. The statement records the church leaders’ assessment of a wider pattern. The sources reviewed leave the perpetrators and motive in the Homs case unresolved.

Why it matters and what to watch

The response gives authorities a concrete test. Can they disclose investigative findings and show how they are protecting clergy and the communities they serve? Watch for a police or prosecutor’s update, confirmed arrests or charges, and documented steps to improve protection. The archbishop’s confirmation that he is safe settles his immediate welfare.
