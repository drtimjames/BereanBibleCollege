Maryland utility lifts water conservation request for 1.9 million customers

WSSC Water says both filtration plants are producing enough drinking water to meet demand after storm-related power disruptions.

What changed

WSSC Water lifted its essential-use-only request at 8:30 a.m. Eastern on Friday, 27 November for customers in Montgomery and Prince George’s counties. The utility said power disruptions had diminished and its Potomac and Patuxent plants were producing safe drinking water at volumes sufficient to meet demand.

Why the utility asked customers to conserve

The request began at 2:30 p.m. Eastern on Thursday, 26 November after a storm interrupted power at both facilities. The initial notice said the Potomac plant was producing water but had intermittent power. The Patuxent plant was out for planned maintenance and also experienced storm-related power disruptions. The utility asked people to conserve water to protect stored reserves and firefighting capacity. Its notice said the water was safe and gave no boil-water instruction.

What to watch

The episode shows how a storm can put pressure on regional water production when power is intermittent and one plant is already undergoing maintenance. WSSC’s statement confirms service recovery. It leaves open how low storage dropped. Will the utility publish an after-action review covering storage levels and power reliability at both plants?
