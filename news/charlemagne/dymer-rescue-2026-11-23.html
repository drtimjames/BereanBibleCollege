Rescuers continue search in Dymer after Russian strike kills 11, Kyiv region says

Kyiv’s regional administration says 11 people died after a drone struck a residential building on 22 November. Rescuers pulled a child and an older woman from the rubble, and the search continued.

What changed

On 23 November local time, regional governor Tymur Tkachenko reported that the death toll had risen to 11. The Kyiv Regional Military Administration said a Russian drone struck a five-story residential building and that search-and-rescue work was still underway. The latest notice gave no remaining count of people believed trapped.

Rescue and relief

Reuters reported that rescuers brought out a seven-year-old boy and a 79-year-old woman alive, citing emergency service spokeswoman Viktoriia Ruban. She said air-raid alerts and the continuing threat of drones interrupted the search. Local authorities opened a support center with social services, volunteers and mental-health support for residents.

Why it matters

Repeated alerts can force rescuers to pause work even when survivors may still be under rubble. Dymer’s response shows the need to sustain rescue and housing support at the same time. The public record establishes the damage to the building and the emergency response. The final casualty count and the extent of displacement remain open.

What to watch

Will authorities declare the search complete and release a final accounting? What temporary housing and repair assistance will be available to residents?
