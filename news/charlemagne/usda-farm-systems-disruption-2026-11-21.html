Outages reported week of 16 November · USDA remarks 20 November · Published 21 November 2026, 7:26 a.m. Pacific

USDA official confirms cyber threat as farm loan and payment systems recover

Federal offices reported disrupted loan and payment tools. An undersecretary said no data was lost and expected most systems back by Friday evening, with weekend work if needed.

What changed

At a press event on 20 November, USDA Undersecretary Richard Fordyce told reporters the disruption involved a cyber threat, DTN reported. Farm Service Agency Administrator Bill Beam had confirmed outages to DTN the day before. The publication reported interruptions to loan software and to tools that release payments and support conservation work. Fordyce said USDA shut down systems as part of its restoration work.

What remains uncertain

Fordyce said the incident caused no loss of data or personally identifiable information. NBC News, as reported by Reuters, cited former officials who described an intrusion into a farmer-payment system. That reporting does not establish what was accessed or taken. The number of producers affected, any payment backlog, and the status of individual services remain to be confirmed. Fordyce’s Friday restoration estimate was a forecast. The sources reviewed give no evidence linking this incident to the Optimizely cases or the other recent outages.

Why it matters and what to watch

Returning software to service and clearing delayed work are separate milestones for producers waiting on loans or payments. Watch for an agency recovery notice identifying which applications are available, guidance on pending transactions, and any later findings or customer notifications that change the assessment of data exposure.
