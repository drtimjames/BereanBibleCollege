European Commission confirms cyber campaign and says most affected applications are back

What changed. The Commission says it detected the campaign on October 22 and confirmed it on October 26. It took several applications offline as a precaution and says most have been restored. The investigation remains open. Spokesperson Thomas Regnier said at the 19 November briefing that the Commission was in contact with CERT-EU and the European Data Protection Supervisor.

Confirmed response and open questions

Regnier said the campaign used AI tools and involved a series of incidents. He declined to identify an attacker or assess the campaign’s scale. He said investigators had found no evidence of a data breach affecting Commission staff as a whole. That statement leaves narrower exposure and the effect on individual applications to be established.

Reuters, citing Politico, reported that personnel and reimbursement applications were among those taken offline. The Commission did not name the affected services in the briefing. Investigators have yet to publish a final impact assessment. The sources reviewed establish no link to the separate Optimizely incident or the NSA and Apple service interruptions.

Why it matters and what to watch

The response itself interrupted administrative services. A restoration notice answers whether applications are available. A separate investigation finding is needed to establish which records, if any, were accessed. Watch for a final service restoration notice, notifications to affected people and an attributable investigation report.
