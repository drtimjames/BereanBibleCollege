Senate passes energy permitting bill 62–33; House action comes next

The Senate bill would shorten some federal project reviews and change who pays for grid upgrades tied to large data centers. It awaits House action.

What changed

On 19 November the U.S. Senate passed H.R. 2347 as amended by a vote of 62–33. A substitute amendment made the bill the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act of 2026. The Senate vote sends the amended measure back to the House. The proposed rules would take effect only if a final version becomes law.

What the Senate bill would do

The measure would set tighter deadlines for some federal environmental reviews, revise parts of transmission planning and limit some challenges to permits. It would also place more of the cost of certain grid upgrades on the large data centers whose power demand prompts them. The vote itself issues no permit, authorizes no specific construction start and puts no new generating capacity into service.

Why it matters

Federal reviews are one of several steps between proposing an energy project and delivering electricity. Shorter reviews could reduce delays for some projects. Financing, equipment supply and grid connection would still shape what gets built and when. Sponsor Sen. Shelley Moore Capito argued the measure would help make energy more affordable. Sen. Jeff Merkley and other critics raised concerns about environmental protections and tribal consultation. The vote establishes no reduction in household bills.

What we’re watching

Will the House accept the Senate text or seek revisions? Which review deadlines and grid-cost provisions would survive in a final bill? If enacted, watch for agency rules and actual project approvals, construction schedules and utility filings.
