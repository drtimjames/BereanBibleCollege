Russian strikes halt Ukrainian power generation and gas production at two sites

DTEK said a thermal plant went offline, and Naftogaz suspended work at a gas production site after a fire. Ukrainian officials reported power outages in five regions.

What changed

On 25 November, Ukrainian power producer DTEK said Russian strikes forced one of its thermal plants offline and badly damaged equipment. It reported no casualties at the plant and withheld its location. Naftogaz separately said a Russian drone set a gas production facility in Poltava region on fire, and the company suspended operations there. It said it would assess the impact once conditions allowed.

Reuters reported that Ukrainian officials counted power outages in five regions. The prime minister separately reported physical damage to energy infrastructure in four regions. An outage can affect a region even if equipment there was undamaged.

Why it matters

The loss of generating capacity and the halt at a gas production site put separate pressures on electricity and heating as winter sets in. DTEK said it was coordinating with grid operator Ukrenergo. The announcements give no estimate of how much supply was lost or when either site can resume operations.

What to watch

Confidence is high in the shutdowns reported by the operators. Look for restoration schedules from Ukrenergo, a damage and restart assessment from Naftogaz, and evidence of how long outages persist in affected communities.
