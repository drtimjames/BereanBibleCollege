New Gulf attack reports: identities and relationship unresolved

Event and reporting: 10 November 2026. UKMTO 201-26 gives 13:38 UTC and 19 nautical miles northeast of Lima, Oman.

Confirmed official report: UKMTO records the tanker master’s report of an unknown projectile strike, crew safe, and no environmental impact reported. Damage assessment was pending first light. This confirms an official incident report, not the attacker’s identity.

Attributed operator statement: ADNOC Logistics & Services says a vessel suffered damage that afternoon in UAE territorial waters. The operator’s own 10 November statement has now been directly checked; WAM, ARN and gCaptain also carry the account. Vessel identity, damage extent and crew condition were not supplied in that statement.

Uncertainty: Do not count these as two distinct attacks until vessel identity and timing are reconciled. No new strait closure or port shutdown is established. No perpetrator is verified.

BRICS connection: The ADNOC-operated vessel establishes direct UAE commercial-shipping exposure. UAE membership does not imply BRICS coordination; the unidentified tanker’s ownership, flag and cargo remain unknown.

Analysis: These reports extend the security problem beyond the previously covered 7 November events and warrant renewed scrutiny of Gulf transit access. Freight-rate changes, cargo losses and capacity reductions cannot yet be quantified. This is incident reporting, not an infrastructure announcement, financing decision or construction milestone.

Follow-up: Obtain vessel names and IMO numbers, first-light damage assessment, crew updates and any operator routing changes. Reconcile ADNOC’s statement with UKMTO before revising incident totals. Biblical interpretation: none proposed.

Sources: UKMTO 201-26 official warning (PDF); ADNOC Logistics & Services, 10 November: directly verified operator statement; WAM reproduction; ARN reproduction; gCaptain, 10 November.

Hormuz traffic: a narrower measure of worsening access

New reporting context — 10 November: Reuters reports S&P Global Energy counted 31 laden LNG tankers exiting Hormuz in October, up from 23 in September; eight October departures loaded in the UAE. This longer-period, LNG-only measure does not contradict the separate weekend commodity-vessel slowdown. UAE-linked exports establish a BRICS-member trade connection; no bloc coordination is implied. Analysis: Partial monthly recovery does not establish safe or unrestricted passage. Follow-up: Reconcile departures with loadings and confirm destination, volume and November continuity. Reuters, 10 November: October LNG traffic; Reuters syndication with figures. Tracking figures remain attributed; underlying data were not independently audited.

Event: 7–8 November 2026. Reporting: Reuters syndication in Gulf Times dated 10 November; reviewed 9 November Pacific time. Displayed publication times differ between indexed copies; exact release time remains unresolved.

Confirmed reporting / attributed data: Reuters cites Kpler recording five commodity-vessel passages on Saturday and none on Sunday, compared with 31 over the preceding weekend. The underlying tracking dataset has not been independently examined here.

BRICS connection: The same account identifies an Indian-flagged gas carrier entering and Iranian fuel oil leaving. This establishes specific India- and Iran-linked shipping exposure, not coordinated BRICS action.

Analysis: Reduced observed movement suggests tighter practical access after tanker attacks. It does not establish a new legal closure, a corresponding percentage loss of cargo volume, or a measured freight-cost increase.

Uncertainty: AIS-dark movements may be missed. Commodity-vessel counts cannot be directly compared with all-vessel totals or U.S.-facilitated movement claims. Do not count this traffic assessment as another attack. Infrastructure announcement, funding and construction are not involved.

Follow-up: Obtain Kpler methodology and revised counts; compare matching dates and vessel classes with JMIC and operator reports. Confirm cargo destinations and whether flows recover before drawing longer-term conclusions.

Sources: Reuters via Gulf Times: shipping slows after tanker attacks; Gulf News: alternative UKMTO/JMIC traffic measures. The latter is contextual reporting, not independent verification of Kpler figures.

HUDA incident: official verification addendum

Official verification update — 10 November 2026: IMO’s confirmed-incident list now includes NAVIG8 MACALLISTER (IMO 9482873), dated 7 November, 8 nautical miles northwest of Khasab, recorded damaged with no pollution. It also adds GRIT (IMO 9243150), dated 3 November, northeast of Lima, damaged with no pollution. These are retrospective confirmations, not two new attacks on 10 November. The Macallister date and location match the already-published Khasab incident; treat linkage as provisional until vessel-level reconciliation. Neither entry identifies the 10 November UKMTO 201 tanker or establishes its connection with ADNOC’s statement. Analysis / follow-up: Amend the existing Hormuz source record rather than duplicate incidents; obtain flag-state or operator confirmation of identity, crew status and onward movement. This is source verification affecting confidence in Iran/UAE-route coverage, not evidence of BRICS coordination or a new closure. IMO confirmed incidents, updated 10 November.

Event: 7 November 2026. Reporting record: IMO list updated 9 November 2026. Prepared for review 9 November 2026; not published.

Confirmed source record: The International Maritime Organization lists HUDA (IMO 9197088) among confirmed regional incidents, records damage and no pollution, and gives its location as 17 nautical miles from Oman’s coast. The IMO describes its compilation as validated with flag/coastal states and verified third-party sources.

Attributed military claim: Earlier reporting attributed disabling the vessel during blockade enforcement to U.S. Central Command. The IMO entry records the consequence, not independent proof of every military allegation about the voyage.

Day Watch analysis / BRICS relevance: This concerns enforcement affecting Iran-linked shipping and access to Iranian ports. Iran’s BRICS membership does not establish bloc involvement. No additional closure, cargo loss, casualties, cost increase or restored vessel operation is established by this entry.

Panama Canal: more booking access, greater permitted draft

Event and reporting date: 9 November 2026. Source: Panama Canal Authority, Advisory A-42-2026, Vice President for Operations Boris Moreno Vásquez.

Confirmed: ACP adds one Panamax Super-category daily booking slot, increasing that category from 18 to 19 and total daily booking capacity to 34. The added slot is offered for transit dates beginning 12 November through staged reservation arrangements. Maximum Neopanamax draft rises immediately to 15.24 metres (50 feet), until further notice.

Authority assessment: ACP attributes the changes to improving water conditions, favorable hydrological projections and water-saving measures. A reservation remains the only guarantee of a transit date; vessels without one may face extensive delays.

Verified BRICS relevance: ACP’s FY2025 annual report ranks China second among canal user countries by cargo tonnage. The connection is Chinese trade exposure, not a BRICS policy or preferential access arrangement.

Day Watch analysis: Another reservation slot improves scheduling access; greater permitted draft can enable eligible ships to carry heavier loads. Lower unit costs are possible, but no freight-rate reduction is established. This is an operating-rule change, not completed infrastructure expansion or a permanent water-supply guarantee.

Charlemagne follow-up: Do auction prices and waiting times fall? Do operators increase cargo loads on China-linked routes? Can the higher draft persist through the next dry season?

Biblical interpretation: No event-specific Scripture connection proposed.
