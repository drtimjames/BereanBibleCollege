HUDA incident: official verification addendum

Event: 7 November 2026. Reporting record: IMO list updated 9 November 2026. Prepared for review 9 November 2026; not published.

Confirmed source record: The International Maritime Organization lists HUDA (IMO 9197088) among confirmed regional incidents, records damage and no pollution, and gives its location as 17 nautical miles from Oman’s coast. The IMO describes its compilation as validated with flag/coastal states and verified third-party sources.

What changed: The earlier report left damage and environmental effects unresolved. This source supplies an official recorded outcome. Do not treat the list’s location as a precise position or silently equate it with UKMTO’s different reference point.

Attributed military claim: Earlier reporting attributed disabling the vessel during blockade enforcement to U.S. Central Command. The IMO entry records the consequence, not independent proof of every military allegation about the voyage.

Day Watch analysis / BRICS relevance: This concerns enforcement affecting Iran-linked shipping and access to Iranian ports. Iran’s BRICS membership does not establish bloc involvement. No additional closure, cargo loss, casualties, cost increase or restored vessel operation is established by this entry.

Integration: Amend the existing 7 November HUDA/UKMTO 198 incident record when preparing the next review batch. Do not add another incident to attack totals. Leave the current live articles unchanged pending approval.

Follow-up: Seek flag-state or owner confirmation of crew condition, damage, cargo, salvage and onward movement. Ask whether the environmental assessment remains current.

Sources: IMO: Highlighted confirmed incidents, updated 9 November 2026; IMO regional incident-validation methodology; Reuters, 7 November: attributed U.S. disabling statement.

Panama Canal: more booking access, greater permitted draft

Event and reporting date: 9 November 2026. Source: Panama Canal Authority, Advisory A-42-2026, Vice President for Operations Boris Moreno Vásquez.

Confirmed: ACP adds one Panamax Super-category daily booking slot, increasing that category from 18 to 19 and total daily booking capacity to 34. The added slot is offered for transit dates beginning 12 November through staged reservation arrangements. Maximum Neopanamax draft rises immediately to 15.24 metres (50 feet), until further notice.

Authority assessment: ACP attributes the changes to improving water conditions, favorable hydrological projections and water-saving measures. A reservation remains the only guarantee of a transit date; vessels without one may face extensive delays.

Verified BRICS relevance: ACP’s FY2025 annual report ranks China second among canal user countries by cargo tonnage. The connection is Chinese trade exposure, not a BRICS policy or preferential access arrangement.

Day Watch analysis: Another reservation slot improves scheduling access; greater permitted draft can enable eligible ships to carry heavier loads. Lower unit costs are possible, but no freight-rate reduction is established. This is an operating-rule change, not completed infrastructure expansion or a permanent water-supply guarantee.

Charlemagne follow-up: Do auction prices and waiting times fall? Do operators increase cargo loads on China-linked routes? Can the higher draft persist through the next dry season?

Biblical interpretation: No event-specific Scripture connection proposed.
