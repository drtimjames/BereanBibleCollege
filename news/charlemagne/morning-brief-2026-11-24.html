The Day Watch Morning Brief

Three vessels continued through Hormuz after being struck, South Korea corrected an island shelter alert, and Estonia set a date to suspend road crossings with Russia.

What did authorities confirm, and what can people and businesses plan around today?

Three vessels struck in Hormuz, UKMTO says

UK Maritime Trade Operations reported on 24 November that three eastbound vessels were struck by unidentified projectiles. All continued their transits, and all crew were accounted for. When this edition was published, two versions of warning 219-26 gave different incident dates, 22 and 23 November. The warning does not establish what struck the ships or how badly they were damaged. At publication, a date clarification and operator reports were still pending.

UKMTO warning 219-26 · UKMTO version listing 22 November · Full report

What changed · 24 November 2026, 12:19 p.m. Pacific. UKMTO reissued its warning and now dates all three strikes to 22 November. It listed three separate incidents involving eastbound tankers. Each continued through the strait and all crew were accounted for. That resolves the date discrepancy in the morning report. The projectile type, damage and environmental effects remain unknown.

South Korea corrects shelter alert and protests Russian flights

On 24 November local time, Russian military aircraft entered South Korea’s air defence identification zone near Ulleung Island. A brief shelter alert initially described a territorial airspace breach. The Joint Chiefs clarified that the aircraft stayed outside territorial airspace. Seoul summoned Russia’s military attaché. A review of the conflicting public alerts would show how officials plan to communicate more clearly next time.

South Korean Defence Ministry · 24 November · Yonhap, citing the Joint Chiefs · 24 November · Full report

Estonia will suspend road crossings with Russia on 1 December

Estonia announced on 20 November that it will suspend crossings at all five road checkpoints. Rail freight can continue under existing controls. Russia summoned Estonia’s chargé d’affaires in protest on 23 November. Estonia’s guidance says the measure will affect some freight routed through Russia to other Eurasian markets. After 1 December, border wait times and rail freight volumes should show where traffic is shifting.

Estonian government decision · 20 November · Estonian freight guidance · Full report

What We’re Watching

  • Hormuz. UKMTO verified the strikes. Watch for its clarification of the date and for ship operators to report damage.
  • Ulleung. Seoul confirmed the protest. Watch for a review of the alert sequence or a diplomatic follow-up.
  • Estonia. The road measure has been adopted. Watch for implementation notices and published freight volumes.
