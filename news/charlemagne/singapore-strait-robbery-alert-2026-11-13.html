Singapore Strait robbery alert follows three reported boardings

ReCAAP reported three incidents aboard eastbound ships in November, with one crew member injured. The corridor carries LNG from Qatar to China. The alert calls for greater vigilance.

What ReCAAP reported

The ReCAAP Information Sharing Centre issued Incident Alert 2/2026 on 11 November. It recorded three incidents on 2, 4 and 10 November involving two bulk carriers and one tanker underway in the Singapore Strait. It reported one crew injury and theft of engine spare parts from two vessels. The incidents brought its year-to-date count for the Straits of Malacca and Singapore to 28.

The centre urged coastal authorities to increase patrols and coordinate responses. It advised ship operators to strengthen watchkeeping and report incidents promptly. The advisory announced no change to transit rules.

Why it matters

China-bound energy cargo uses this corridor. The U.S. Energy Information Administration estimates that Qatar-to-China shipments accounted for 28 percent of LNG volumes through the Strait of Malacca in the first half of 2025. That historical figure establishes China’s exposure to the route. The alert does not identify the cargoes, destinations or commercial delays associated with the three ships.

The cluster makes crew safety and shipboard security an immediate concern. Commercial consequences would depend on whether incidents recur and whether operators change sailing schedules or insurers change terms. The available reports do not establish a common perpetrator or a sustained increase in risk per voyage.

What to watch next

  • Do coastal authorities confirm additional patrols or arrests?
  • Do subsequent reports show more boardings relative to traffic, or changes to schedules, insurance or cargo delivery?
