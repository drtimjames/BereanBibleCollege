Delta restores flight-dispatch system after Atlanta ground stop and more than 1,600 delays

Delta restored its aircraft weight-and-balance system on 22 November after a carrier-specific ground stop for Atlanta-bound flights. The airline reported more than 1,600 delays nationwide.

What happened on 22 November

FAA advisory 042 shows that the ground stop applied to Delta and its affiliates on covered departures bound for Hartsfield–Jackson Atlanta International Airport. The agency issued it at the carrier’s request. A ground delay program succeeded the stop, then the FAA canceled that program at 11:28 a.m. Eastern after the carrier’s traffic-management objectives were met.

Delta said it had used manual procedures to keep dispatching flights while it restored automated weight-and-balance calculations. The airline later said the system was operating normally and reported more than 1,600 delays nationwide, ABC News reported. The FAA said the delays were unrelated to its air traffic systems. Delta said the outage was unrelated to a hack.

Why it matters

Aircraft weight and balance are checked before departure. Manual procedures allowed some flights to leave while the airline restored automation, but delays persisted after the technology recovered. The traffic restriction was specific to the carrier and its Atlanta hub. The underlying technical failure and the timing of full schedule recovery remain open.

What to watch

How quickly do cancellations and delays return to baseline? Will Delta release a technical explanation or changes to its dispatch contingency procedures?
