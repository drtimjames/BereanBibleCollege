FCC overrides California rules that would block AT&T’s copper-phone transition

The ruling clears a regulatory obstacle in parts of California. AT&T’s applications list June 2027 as the earliest planned cutoff, and replacement service varies by address.

What changed

On 20 November the Federal Communications Commission released a ruling limiting California’s ability to enforce state service requirements that would impede AT&T’s federally approved move away from legacy copper voice service in affected areas. The Los Angeles Times and Ars Technica reported that the decision follows earlier FCC approval of AT&T’s applications to discontinue those services.

The California Public Utilities Commission says AT&T’s applications cover portions of 360 California wire centers. AT&T estimates that approximately 184,000 residential and 15,000 business customers are affected, with retirement proposed on or after 1 June 2027. The November ruling is a regulatory decision. Actual service changes depend on customer notices and implementation.

Why it matters for emergency communications

AT&T told the Los Angeles Times it would preserve access to voice and 911 service during the transition. FCC Commissioner Anna Gomez dissented and said California should have time to finish its review of basic service requirements. A replacement service’s ability to place calls during an outage depends on local network coverage and whether customer equipment has power. The FCC’s earlier backup-power order explains why this matters for fixed voice services that do not draw power from the line.

What we’re watching

Which addresses receive notices, when will service change and what replacement equipment and backup power will be available? Watch for specific AT&T transition notices, any subsequent state or court action and evidence that replacement voice service remains available during local outages.
