Humanitarian access · Event and UN statement 22 November 2026 · Published 23 November 2026, 3:53 p.m. Pacific

UN says its Khartoum premises were damaged during drone strikes

The UN’s humanitarian coordinator said staff were inside when a UN building in a civilian neighborhood was damaged on 22 November. The UN statement gave no staff casualty count or attribution.

What happened

Denise Brown, the UN’s resident and humanitarian coordinator in Sudan, said drones targeted Khartoum early on 22 November and a UN site in a civilian neighborhood was damaged with staff inside. Her statement gave no damage assessment, staff casualty count or identification of the party responsible. It did not establish whether the premises were deliberately targeted.

Why it matters

Brown said the UN’s humanitarian workers were in Sudan to assist people across the country and called attention to the protections afforded humanitarian personnel and facilities. Damage to an aid facility could disrupt staff movements or interrupt programs. The UN has yet to report any suspension or relocation tied to this incident in the statements reviewed.

What to watch

Will the UN account for the staff who were inside, describe the damage, and confirm whether aid operations can continue from the site? Will investigators publish evidence identifying who was responsible?
