UK warns suppliers to Ukraine’s armed forces of Russian sabotage risk

The UK’s protective security authority and Defence Ministry warned organizations supplying Ukraine’s military. Ministers announced separate briefings for defence and critical infrastructure leaders on 24 and 25 November.

What the UK announced

The National Protective Security Authority, part of MI5, issued a joint alert with the Ministry of Defence on 24 November. UK officials said the Russian state was targeting British organizations that supply Ukraine’s armed forces. The Cabinet Office said the armed forces minister convened defence executives on Tuesday and the security minister planned to convene critical infrastructure executives on Wednesday. It said the sessions were closed and would provide tailored security advice.

The government cited an increase in attempted sabotage against European defence-related facilities and advised companies to strengthen protection of people, sites and supply chains. The public statement gives no list of targeted firms and identifies no newly completed UK attack. The Associated Press independently reported the alert and briefings.

Why it matters

A supplier disruption can delay delivery to Ukraine even when the end customer is outside the UK. The briefings may help companies review whether they can keep operating if one facility or supplier is disrupted. The public release gives no evidence of delivery stoppages or a connection to the reported 13 November activity at RAF Lakenheath.

What to watch

Will the government publish any follow-up guidance after the infrastructure briefing? Do police, prosecutors or suppliers report specific incidents or interruptions to deliveries?
