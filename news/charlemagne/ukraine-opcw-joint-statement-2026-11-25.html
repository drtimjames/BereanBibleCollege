Joint statement calls for accountability over riot control agents in Ukraine

Diplomats called for an end to their use as a method of warfare. OPCW-designated labs confirmed a riot control agent in samples. Attribution is a separate question.

What the statement says

Non-proliferation directors from the United Kingdom, the European Union, Ukraine and other governments issued a joint statement on 25 November calling for an immediate halt to the use of riot control agents as a method of warfare in Ukraine. They called for identifying perpetrators through the Organisation for the Prohibition of Chemical Weapons (OPCW) and the Chemical Weapons Convention.

What the evidence establishes

The OPCW circulated Ukraine’s national investigation report to member states on 17 November. Its technical work confirmed the presence of a riot control agent in samples. Ukraine’s report attributes documented incidents to Russian forces. The joint statement calls for further attribution work, and the OPCW sample findings do not independently establish who was responsible for each incident.

Why it matters and what to watch

The convention prohibits the use of riot control agents as a method of warfare. The joint statement shows diplomatic support for pursuing accountability. Watch for a formal OPCW decision, published attribution findings or a response from Russia to this statement.
