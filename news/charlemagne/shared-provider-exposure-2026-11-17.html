Optimizely confirms malicious website code; researcher reports exposure across 178 organizations

A shared website service delivered hostile code to visitors. The scale of website exposure and the evidence of actual loss require separate accounting.

What changed · 17 November 2026, 12:41 p.m. Pacific. A vendor-confirmed security incident and the researcher’s technical evidence establish a malicious-script exposure distinct from the NSA and Apple service outages. Visa separately describes an incident at an Asia-Pacific provider.

Vendor confirmation and cleanup

Optimizely’s incident notice, posted at 01:02 UTC on 17 November (5:02 p.m. Pacific on 16 November), says an unauthorized party exploited an Experimentation vulnerability to add malicious JavaScript to customer projects. The company says it fixed the vulnerability, removed the injected code and secured affected accounts. Its notice remained at Monitoring when checked.

Original Optimizely incident notice

What the research establishes

Scott Helme’s Report URI investigation, dated 16 November, reports 188 infected snippets among 1,611 examined, associated with 178 organizations. Those are sampled website exposures; they do not establish 178 separate internal-network breaches.

Visa: a separate third-party incident

Visa told Cybernews the incident involved an Asia-Pacific service provider. The spokesperson said Visa’s systems were unaffected and there was no indication payment data, including card numbers, was affected. The provider and exposed records were not identified. Criminal group n0n’s extortion claims are allegations, not verified findings about Visa or other named companies. No link to Optimizely is established.

Cybernews, including Visa’s directly obtained statement · 17 November

The Charlemagne Assessment

Assessment confidence: high in the attributed Optimizely acknowledgment; moderate in the researcher’s measured exposure scope, which Day Watch has not independently reproduced. The vendor’s cleanup addresses delivery of the injected code. It cannot establish what visitors lost before removal.

Shared services can concentrate risk across otherwise independent websites. The operational questions concern which pages loaded affected scripts, what data those pages handled and which protections limited access. Organization-specific findings could substantially narrow or enlarge the eventual harm. Simultaneous outages elsewhere supply no evidence of a common attacker, campaign or geopolitical sponsor.

Why it matters and next evidence

Readers depend on banking, travel, commerce and communication sites whose pages can load outside code. A familiar site name alone does not identify which visitor sessions were exposed. Follow vendor root-cause findings, affected-project notifications and organization-specific disclosures. Which sessions executed the code? What theft is independently confirmed? Have affected sessions and accounts been secured?
