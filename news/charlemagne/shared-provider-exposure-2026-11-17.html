Optimizely confirms malicious website code; researcher reports exposure across 178 organizations

A shared website service delivered hostile code to visitors. The scale of website exposure and the evidence of actual loss require separate accounting.

What changed · 18 November 2026, 9:44 a.m. Pacific. In its 18 November status update, Optimizely said fewer than 5% of its customers were affected and that it had contacted every Web Experimentation customer to confirm individual impact. The vendor percentage and Report URI’s count of observed organizations describe different populations. Visitor losses remain unresolved. Optimizely status update

What changed · 17 November 2026, 12:41 p.m. Pacific. A vendor-confirmed security incident and the researcher’s technical evidence establish a malicious-script exposure distinct from the NSA and Apple service outages. Visa separately describes an incident at an Asia-Pacific provider.

What changed · 17 November 2026, 7:17 p.m. Pacific. Optimizely’s initial customer report narrows the exposure to Web Experimentation and documents the cleanup timeline. The company says the injected code was removed from all affected projects by 22:24 UTC on 16 November (2:24 p.m. Pacific). It reports no evidence of customer password exposure. That finding leaves open what information the code may have collected from visitors’ browsers before removal.

Optimizely’s initial customer incident report · 17 November (PDF)

Salesforce’s website advisory remains open

What changed in this report · 18 November 2026, 10:30 p.m. Pacific. Salesforce’s advisory, dated 17 November UTC and still marked ongoing when checked, says the company is investigating unauthorized changes to Optimizely scripts used on certain Salesforce websites. It says it is verifying that the scripts are disabled across preview and production environments as a precaution. The advisory provides no finding of unauthorized access to customer data. It does not expand the vendor’s affected-customer count. Watch for a Salesforce notice identifying affected sites or visitor data and the outcome of its investigation.

Salesforce Trust advisory 20000274 · 17 November

What changed · 19 November 2026, 12:36 a.m. Pacific. A separate Salesforce statement dated 18 November confirms the precaution was implemented. Salesforce says it disabled Optimizely scripts on its sites and found no evidence of impact to its platform or subscription services. Its investigation into any visitor or customer-data impact remains open. Salesforce distinguishes its own exposed website scripts from hosted customer sites, where exposure to this incident depends on whether the site owner used the affected Optimizely product. Salesforce Security statement · 18 November

Vendor confirmation and cleanup

Optimizely’s incident notice, posted at 01:02 UTC on 17 November (5:02 p.m. Pacific on 16 November), says an unauthorized party exploited an Experimentation vulnerability to add malicious JavaScript to customer projects. The company says it fixed the vulnerability, removed the injected code and secured affected accounts. Its notice was at Monitoring at the time of our initial report.

What the research establishes

Scott Helme’s Report URI investigation, dated 16 November, reports 188 infected snippets among 1,611 examined, associated with 178 organizations. Those are sampled website exposures; they do not establish 178 separate internal-network breaches.

Visa: a separate third-party incident

Visa told Cybernews the incident involved an Asia-Pacific service provider. The spokesperson said Visa’s systems were unaffected and there was no indication payment data, including card numbers, was affected. The provider and exposed records were not identified. Criminal group n0n’s extortion claims are allegations, not verified findings about Visa or other named companies. No link to Optimizely is established.

The Charlemagne Assessment

Assessment confidence: high in the attributed Optimizely acknowledgment; moderate in the researcher’s measured exposure scope, which Day Watch has not independently reproduced. The vendor’s cleanup addresses delivery of the injected code. It cannot establish what visitors lost before removal.

Shared services can concentrate risk across otherwise independent websites. The operational questions concern which pages loaded affected scripts, what data those pages handled and which protections limited access. Organization-specific findings could substantially narrow or enlarge the eventual harm. Simultaneous outages elsewhere supply no evidence of a common attacker, campaign or geopolitical sponsor.

Why it matters and next evidence

Readers depend on banking, travel, commerce and communication sites whose pages can load outside code. A familiar site name alone does not identify which visitor sessions were exposed. Follow vendor root-cause findings, affected-project notifications and organization-specific disclosures. Which sessions executed the code? What theft is independently confirmed? Have affected sessions and accounts been secured?
