Three Jehovah’s Witnesses receive prison terms in Russian-occupied Crimea

A Simferopol court convicted four members of the religious group on 25 November. Three received terms of up to eight years, and a fourth received a suspended sentence, the group said.

What changed

Jehovah’s Witnesses reported on 26 November that the Simferopol District Court had convicted four members a day earlier in Russian-occupied Crimea. The group said three defendants received prison terms of eight, seven and a half, and six and a half years. The fourth received a three-year suspended sentence. Reuters reported the outcome on 27 November, citing the group’s spokesperson, and Russian rights monitor OVD-Info also reported the prison terms.

What the case concerns

Russian authorities prosecuted the defendants under extremism provisions. Russia banned the organization in 2017. The defendants said their activity was peaceful religious practice, according to the group’s spokesperson. In 2022, the European Court of Human Rights ruled that earlier Russian measures against Jehovah’s Witnesses violated Convention rights. That judgment addressed earlier cases.

Why it matters and what to watch

The sentences show how Russia’s ban is being enforced in Crimea and what prison terms can result from an extremism prosecution involving religious practice. Watch for a published judgment, any appeal and the defendants’ custody status.
