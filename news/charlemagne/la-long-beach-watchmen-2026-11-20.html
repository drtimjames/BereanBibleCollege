LA–Long Beach port watchmen contract nears expiry ahead of Saturday deadline

Employers and ILWU Local 26 scheduled talks before the 12:01 a.m. Pacific deadline. A work stoppage or terminal closure had yet to be confirmed.

What changed

The Pacific Maritime Association (PMA) said bargaining on 17 November ended without a deal for about 250 security watchmen at six container terminals in Los Angeles and Long Beach. In its 18 November statement, the employer group said ILWU Local 26 had authorized a strike, was unwilling to extend the contract again and had agreed to return to the bargaining table on 20 November. The current extension is due to expire at 12:01 a.m. Pacific on 21 November. APL Logistics warned customers on 19 November of possible interruptions and said no strike was underway at the time.

Reuters reported that the union did not immediately comment on PMA’s account. At publication, a Friday bargaining outcome, a walkout and any resulting terminal closure remained unconfirmed. PMA said federal mediators had helped with talks and warned that a stoppage could spread if other workers honor picket lines.

Why it matters for Pacific trade

The watchmen cover gate security and other duties at terminals that handle a substantial share of both ports’ cargo, according to PMA. Restaffing or closing a gate could affect truck appointments and onward rail moves. The Port of Los Angeles ranked China and Hong Kong first among its trading partners in 2025, with roughly $82 billion in trade. That figure describes the port’s annual trade. The amount of China-linked cargo at risk this weekend remains unknown.

What we’re watching

Will the two sides reach a deal or extend the contract again? If workers walk out, which terminals publish gate or vessel notices, and do rail or drayage operators confirm delays? Those notices would establish the impact on cargo movement.
