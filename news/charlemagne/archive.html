Strategic Watch — 13 November 2026

FAA lifts nationwide ground stop after communications outage

The FAA said traffic resumed at 8:15 p.m. Eastern on 13 November and counted about 12,462 cancellations that day across all causes. The root cause of the communications failure remains under investigation. Flight disruptions eased on 16 November. The Wall Street Journal reported on 17 November, citing an FAA official, that router installations had been paused during the investigation.

Lower Mississippi reopens with restrictions after tanker collision

The Coast Guard’s salvage safety zone runs through 11 December unless cancelled earlier. The Coast Guard lifted the full closure on the evening of 13 November, with one-way traffic and a safety zone remaining near the New Orleans collision site. On 15 November, an NTSB official said the tanker lost steering. Investigators are still determining why. Everyone aboard survived. City officials said four people remained hospitalized on 16 November.

Suspected drone activity prompts RAF Lakenheath security response

The U.S. Air Force said the response ended without impact to personnel, aircraft or infrastructure.

Pasifik and Xi’an rail operator sign Middle Corridor freight agreement

Pasifik plans more China–Türkiye freight via the Caspian and reports a separate deal to raise its Europe–Türkiye schedule from four to seven weekly trains. Start dates remain unconfirmed.

Minsk-area rail terminal opens with China-bound export freight

GREATPORT opened on 6 November. Its rail partner reported a test arrival from Xi’an and a China-bound export train routed via Russia and Kazakhstan.

Singapore Strait robbery alert follows three reported boardings

ReCAAP reported three incidents aboard eastbound ships in November, with one crew member injured. The corridor carries LNG from Qatar to China. The alert calls for greater vigilance.

Strategic Watch — 12 November 2026

UK sanctions Vostok Oil, Russia’s Arctic crude supplier to China

The UK designation adds asset and transport restrictions to a Russian Arctic export project whose crude has reached China. The effect on shipments remains unknown.

Strategic Watch — 11 November 2026

Hormuz LNG shipments rose in October as attacks on ships increased 12 November update: tanker crew evacuated

S&P Global counted 31 outbound LNG cargoes in October, up from 23 in September. The recovery varied sharply by exporter, while attacks on ships increased.
