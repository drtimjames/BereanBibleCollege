The Day Watch Morning Brief

Finland prepares a tow for Sparta II, COP31 closes without an overarching agreement, and a China–Mediterranean shipping service reaches its first scheduled departure date.

What evidence will show when a local emergency is contained, a shipping route is operating, or a diplomatic commitment takes effect?

Finland keeps cooling damaged Russian cargo ship as tow preparations advance

On November 22, Finland’s Border Guard said Finnish, Estonian and Russian units were working to stabilize Sparta II. Officials said visible flames were out but hot spots remained, and preparations continued for Russian vessels to tow the ship. Reuters reported that all 16 crew had evacuated after the November 21 strike, with one slightly injured. Watch for a confirmed handover, any pollution report and Finland’s notice lifting the local safety zone.

COP31 ends without an overarching climate agreement

At the November 22 close in Antalya, conference president Murat Kurum said there was no agreement on the summit’s overarching decision. The European Union and Panama opposed the final draft, and Australia’s lead negotiator identified fossil-fuel language and climate finance as sticking points. AP reported progress on separate issues. The outcome leaves those disputes open. Watch for the published decisions and for any governments to put forward funded commitments of their own.

A new China–Mediterranean service reaches its first scheduled departure date

Maersk’s October 19 notice scheduled November 22 for the first Shanghai departure on its new AE18 service, calling at Xiamen, Malaysia’s Tanjung Pelepas, Algeciras and Tangier. The service is part of its reshaped network after the announced Suez rerouting. The first departure and subsequent canal transit still need confirmation. Flexport also projected a late-November surge in scheduled North European arrival capacity, giving shippers a reason to check berth and inland schedules as routes change.

Hormuz traffic update · 22 November 2026, 6:25 p.m. Pacific

What changed. JMIC cited 114 U.S.-assisted crossings on 19–21 November, about 38 a day compared with 27 a day in its prior window. Its separate tracking series found zero eligible AIS-visible non-facilitated cargo crossings. That split suggests assisted passage increased during the short window while independent traffic remained difficult to observe. What would change the assessment is sustained passage across comparable windows and verified deliveries to buyers.

Baltic restrictions lifted · 22 November 2026, 5:56 p.m. Pacific

What changed. Finland’s Gulf of Finland Coast Guard said the tow left Finland’s exclusive economic zone and pollution-response area. It lifted the local sea and air restrictions and ended its mission. The update ends the Finnish restrictions around the casualty. The next evidence is confirmation of the ship’s arrival and condition.

Baltic rescue update · 22 November 2026, 4:37 p.m. Pacific

What changed. Helsinki’s rescue department said Finland ended its heightened oil-spill standby. The maritime salvage handover and the status of the local safety zone remain open. The downgrade reduces emergency staging in Finland while the maritime authorities track the tow.

Update · 22 November 2026, 8:15 a.m. Pacific

What changed. Finland’s Border Guard confirmed that the Russian rescue vessel Murman began towing Sparta II. The handover and the status of the local safety zone remained unconfirmed. The watch now shifts from whether the ship can be moved to whether the handover is completed without pollution.

Aviation update · 22 November 2026, 3:19 p.m. Pacific

What changed. Delta restored its aircraft weight-and-balance system on 22 November after a carrier-specific ground stop for Atlanta-bound flights. The airline reported more than 1,600 delays nationwide. Manual dispatch procedures kept some flights moving. The FAA said its air traffic systems were unaffected. Watch for the airline’s schedule recovery and technical findings.
