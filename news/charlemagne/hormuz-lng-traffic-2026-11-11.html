11 November 2026 · Maritime trade

Hormuz LNG shipments rose in October as attacks on ships increased

S&P Global counted 31 outbound LNG cargoes in October, up from 23 in September. The recovery varied sharply by exporter, while attacks on ships increased.

Tanker crew evacuated after projectile strike near the UAE

12 November update. UK Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO) reported that a tanker was struck by an unidentified projectile on 11 November while leaving the Strait of Hormuz near Ras Al Khaimah. The strike caused an engine-room fire. All crew were evacuated safely, and firefighting was ongoing at the time of the notice.

The incident adds to evidence of risk for commercial shipping on the corridor used by UAE exporters. The tanker’s owner, cargo and destination remain unknown. Iran’s military separately claimed that four tankers were hit by explosions. Reuters could not verify that account. The relationship between the UKMTO report and Iran’s claim, and any change in commercial access, remain unconfirmed.

What the October data show

S&P Global reported on 2 November that October crossings reached a three-month high, supported by dry bulk and LNG, while very large crude carrier traffic declined. In its 11 November briefing, Reuters cited S&P Global Energy’s count of 31 laden LNG tanker departures through the strait in October, versus 23 in September. An S&P analyst told Reuters that UAE LNG traffic had recovered to prewar levels and Qatar’s was just above 30 percent of its prewar level. Reuters separately cited a Washington Institute count of 37 vessels hit in October. The attack tally covers vessels more broadly than the LNG departure count.

Why it matters

The UAE figures establish a direct BRICS trade connection through a member country’s LNG shipments. Qatar provides a separate comparison. Rising departures show that more LNG moved through Hormuz during October, while the uneven recovery and attack tally make clear that shipment counts alone cannot establish safe or predictable access.

What remains uncertain

Iranian state media claimed on 10 November that navigation systems on Oman’s Didamar Island were destroyed. On 11 November Reuters reported corroboration of a strike from three sources, but could not independently determine whether any systems were destroyed. The effect on commercial navigation remains unverified. See the 10 November Gulf vessel incident report for the separate vessel cases.
